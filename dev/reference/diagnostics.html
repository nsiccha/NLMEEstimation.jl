Local uncertainty ​

NLMEEstimation.covariance_step Function
julia
covariance_step(model, fit::FOCEResult; method = :rs, step = 1e-3,
                approximation = FOCEApproximation(interaction = fit.options.interaction))
covariance_step(model, fit::Union{SAEMResult, RaggedSAEMResult}; method = :rs,
                step = 1e-3, approximation = <see below>, rng = Random.default_rng())
covariance_step(model, θ, σ, ω; subjects = 1:nsubjects(model), free = nothing,
                η0 = nothing, method = :rs, step = 1e-3,
                approximation = FOCEApproximation(), rng = Random.default_rng(),
                progress = nothing)
    -> CovarianceResult

The covariance step: the sampling covariance and standard errors of the estimated population coordinates (θ, σ, ω), in the unconstrained coordinates in which they are fitted.

R is minus the Hessian of the fitted objective and S the sum over subjects of the outer products of their marginal log-likelihood gradients. method = :rs (the default) returns the sandwich R⁻¹ S R⁻¹, which remains consistent when the model's random-effect or residual distribution is misspecified; :r returns the inverse observed information R⁻¹, and :s the inverse cross-product S⁻¹. With a population_logprior, R includes the prior and R⁻¹ is the Laplace approximation of the population posterior covariance at the MAP point.

Both matrices are central finite differences of the per-subject marginal log-likelihoods of approximation, which needs objective values only: no population derivatives of the model. Each coordinate j moves by step * max(1, |x_j|). :s takes 2p + 1 evaluations for p estimated coordinates; :r and :rs take 2p² + 1. Every evaluation warm-starts the subject modes from the same solution at the estimate. With LouisInformation both matrices come instead from posterior draws of the random effects at the point (Louis's identity), and step only differences the population gradient for the curvature in θ and σ. A model with provides_laplace_gradient takes AdaptiveGaussHermite(nodes = 1) from its own laplace_marginals_and_gradient: R is then the central difference of that gradient and S comes from the per-subject values at the same points, 2p + 1 calls for every method. TMB's sdreport takes R the same way.

For a FOCE(I) fit, the defaults reuse its subjects, its fitted coordinates (fit.free) and its interaction setting, so the step differentiates the fitted objective. For an SAEM fit, all (θ, σ, ω) are estimated. A model with GaussianObservations and an NLMELayout defaults to the FOCE(I) linearization at the SAEM estimate. A model with a RaggedNLMELayout defaults to the Laplace approximation, AdaptiveGaussHermite(nodes = 1), when it reports η Hessians (its own blocks, or a declared eta_hessian_sparsity: the rule default_ebe_method uses for Newton) or supplies its Laplace gradient, and to LouisInformation otherwise. Any other model defaults to ImportanceSampling. Its draws, from rng, are held fixed across the difference points, so the estimated marginal is a smooth function of the coordinates, and perturbations of ω and of mu-referenced θ reuse the conditional values and cost no model evaluations. AdaptiveGaussHermite is the deterministic alternative. It adapts at every difference point, finding the subject modes and curvature there again (warm-started from the estimate's, gtol = 1e-10), so nodes = 1 gives the Laplace covariance, the one subjects with many random effects need. free selects positions in the estimated coordinates [θ; σ; free Ω coordinates], which are the concatenated (θ, σ, ω) unless the model declares fixed_omega (see CovarianceResult). Fixed covariance parts carry no uncertainty and are never differentiated; ω must hold their values. Throws CovarianceStepError when the requested matrix is not positive definite.

progress (a Treebars node or backend symbol) gets a Covariance step node: the subject modes at the point, then a Marginal likelihoods counter over the 2p² + 1 (or 2p + 1) evaluations, or, with the model's Laplace gradient, a Laplace gradients counter over its 2p + 1 calls. An interrupt raises Treebars.ProgressInterrupted.

NLMEEstimation.CovarianceResult Type
julia
CovarianceResult

Result of covariance_step. point is the full concatenated population vector [θ; σ; ω] at which the step was taken, in the model's unconstrained coordinates and the package's log-Cholesky ω. coordinates is the same point in the coordinates the estimators fit: [θ; σ; free Ω coordinates]. It equals point unless the model declares fixed_omega; then a fully fixed family has no coordinates, and a partially fixed family contributes its estimated log standard deviations (named log sd(…)) and, when its correlation is estimated, its correlation coordinates m_jl = L_jl / L_jj of the Cholesky factor L (named m(…)). Every other vector and matrix field is indexed by the estimated coordinates free (positions in coordinates), named in names:

  • covariance, standard_errors: the estimated sampling covariance of those coordinates and the square roots of its diagonal, for method :rs (R⁻¹ S R⁻¹), :r (R⁻¹) or :s (S⁻¹).

  • R: minus the Hessian of the fitted objective, the summed marginal log-likelihood plus any population_logprior (objective is :ML or :MAP); nothing for method = :s, which does not compute it.

  • S: Σᵢ gᵢ gᵢᵀ over the subjects' marginal log-likelihood gradients gᵢ, the columns of subject_gradients. A population prior is not a subject term and enters R only.

  • gradient: the gradient of the fitted objective, close to zero at a converged estimate.

  • step: the central-difference step per coordinate; evaluations counts the marginal-approximation evaluations, or the calls of laplace_marginals_and_gradient where the model supplies it.

Use delta_method for natural-unit quantities.

source
NLMEEstimation.CovarianceStepError Type
julia
CovarianceStepError

Thrown by covariance_step when the requested covariance does not exist: R is not positive definite (methods :rs and :r) or S is singular (method :s, for example with fewer subjects than estimated coordinates). The fields R, S, gradient and names hold what was computed, for diagnosis.

NLMEEstimation.FOCEApproximation Type
julia
FOCEApproximation(; interaction = true, ebe_method = nothing,
                    ebe_options = EBEOptions(gtol = 1e-10))

The FOCE(I) approximation of each subject's marginal log-likelihood, the per-subject terms of focei_evaluate, as used by covariance_step. interaction = false gives FOCE without interaction. ebe_method = nothing selects default_ebe_method: Newton when the model provides η Hessian blocks, otherwise BFGS. The default mode tolerance is tighter than a fit's because finite differences of the objective amplify the error of the inner mode search. Requires GaussianObservations and an NLMELayout.

NLMEEstimation.delta_method Function
julia
delta_method(result::CovarianceResult, f; jacobian = nothing)
    -> (estimate, covariance, standard_errors)

First-order (delta-method) uncertainty of natural-unit quantities f(x), a real vector function of the full population vector x = [θ; σ; ω] (result.point). jacobian(x) may supply ∂f/∂x as a length(f(x)) × length(x) matrix; otherwise the Jacobian is taken by central differences. Coordinates outside result.free, and covariance parts held by fixed_omega, carry no uncertainty; the Jacobian is taken with respect to result.coordinates, mapped through the fixed parts. For example, with nθ = length(θ) and nσ = length(σ), the covariance of exp.(θ) and of the Ω entries is

julia
delta_method(result, x -> exp.(x[1:nθ]))
delta_method(result, x -> omega_elements(layout, x[nθ+nσ+1:end]))

NLMEEstimation.omega_elements Function
julia
omega_elements(layout, ω) -> Vector

The entries of Ω on the covariance scale, in one-to-one correspondence with the log-Cholesky coordinates ω: block by block (family by family for a RaggedNLMELayout), the lower triangle in row-major order. Diagonal positions are variances and the others covariances. Pair it with delta_method for standard errors of Ω.

NLMEEstimation.LouisInformation Type
julia
LouisInformation(; draws = 1000, burnin = 200, kernel = WarmupHMCNUTS())

covariance_step's information matrices by Louis's missing-information identity (Louis 1982), from posterior draws of the random effects at the point: SAEM's stochastic-approximation Fisher information (Kuhn & Lavielle 2005; Monolix's default, nlmixr2's SAEM "sa"). For subject i, with ψ the estimated coordinates and draws η_i ~ p(η_i | y_i, ψ),

julia
-∂²ψ log p(y_i | ψ) = E[-∂²ψ log p(y_i, η_i | ψ)] - Var[∂ψ log p(y_i, η_i | ψ)]
 ∂ψ log p(y_i | ψ)  = E[∂ψ log p(y_i, η_i | ψ)]

so R (the summed first line) and S (the outer products of the second) need neither a marginal likelihood nor the curvature in η. The complete-data log density is taken in the mu-referenced coordinates φ = η + Xθ_μ, in which the model depends on the mu-referenced θ only through the Gaussian prior. The derivatives in those θ and in ω are closed form. Those in the other θ and in σ come from conditional_loglikelihood_and_population_gradient (one call per subject and draw), and their curvature from central differences of the summed population gradient (two batched calls per coordinate and draw, with covariance_step's step). That gradient takes the route saem_population_gradient_style names: summed per-subject calls, or conditional_loglikelihood_sum_and_population_gradient for the batch.

kernel, a WarmupHMCNUTS, draws each subject's η: Pathfinder from the starting η (a fit's chain states or modes), burnin discarded transitions that adapt the kernel, then draws kept ones under the kernel frozen. R and S carry Monte Carlo error that falls as 1/√draws. WarmupHMCNUTS(threads = true) takes the draws on Julia's threads; the scores and curvature between them stay on the calling thread. It applies to any likelihood and to both layouts, asks the model for no η Hessian, and keeps no n × n matrix per subject. It is not a marginal likelihood: marginal_loglikelihood refuses it.

NLMEEstimation.marginal_loglikelihood Function
julia
marginal_loglikelihood(model, fit; approximation = <see below>,
                       rng = Random.default_rng(), nobs = nothing, progress = nothing)
marginal_loglikelihood(model, θ, σ, ω; subjects = 1:nsubjects(model), η0 = nothing,
                       approximation = <see below>, rng = Random.default_rng(),
                       nparameters = nothing, nobs = nothing, progress = nothing)
    -> MarginalLikelihoodResult

The marginal log-likelihood Σᵢ log ∫ p(y_i | θ, σ, η) N(η; 0, Ω) dη at a fit's estimate or at given population coordinates, with OFV, AIC and BIC. This gives SAEM fits a likelihood-based objective. approximation is ImportanceSampling, AdaptiveGaussHermite or FOCEApproximation. The first two need only conditional values and η gradients, so they apply to any likelihood and to ragged layouts. The default is ImportanceSampling(), except on a RaggedNLMELayout whose model reports η Hessians (its own blocks, or a declared eta_hessian_sparsity): there it is the Laplace approximation, AdaptiveGaussHermite(nodes = 1), because importance sampling loses effective draws as a subject grows. Subject modes warm-start from the fit's EBEs or final chain states (η0). nparameters, the AIC/BIC parameter count, defaults to the estimated coordinates: θ, σ and the Ω coordinates that fixed_omega leaves free, or fit.free for a FOCE(I) fit. nobs overrides the observation count used by BIC. progress (a Treebars node or backend symbol) gets a Marginal likelihood node with the subject modes and, while they are evaluated, a counter over the sampling or quadrature points; an interrupt raises Treebars.ProgressInterrupted.

source
NLMEEstimation.MarginalLikelihoodResult Type
julia
MarginalLikelihoodResult

Result of marginal_loglikelihood. point is [θ; σ; ω]. loglikelihood is the marginal log-likelihood with every constant kept, and subject_loglikelihoods[k] is subject subjects[k]'s term. standard_error and subject_standard_errors are Monte Carlo standard errors. They are zero for the deterministic approximations, which report no approximation error. effective_sample_size holds the per-subject importance sampling effective sample sizes, or nothing.

logposterior adds a model population_logprior once (objective :MAP). ofv is -2 logposterior, as in FOCEResult, and equals -2 loglikelihood under :ML. nlmixr2's OBJF omits the Gaussian constant: it is ofv - nobs * log(2π). aic = -2 loglikelihood + 2 nparameters and bic = -2 loglikelihood + nparameters * log(nobs), where nobs counts the observations of the listed subjects. nobs, and so bic, is missing for a model that declares no observation_model unless it is passed explicitly. evaluations counts batched conditional evaluations (missing for FOCEApproximation).

NLMEEstimation.ImportanceSampling Type
julia
ImportanceSampling(; draws = 2000, df = 4, scale = 1.0)

Importance-sampling estimate of each subject's marginal log-likelihood log ∫ p(y_i | θ, σ, η) N(η; 0, Ω) dη. The proposal for subject i is a multivariate Student t with df degrees of freedom (a positive integer, or Inf for a Gaussian), centred at the subject's conditional mode, with scale matrix scale² times the inverse curvature of the complete-data log-density there. The curvature comes from the η Hessian blocks when the model provides them, and otherwise from central differences of batched η gradients. Each draw costs one batched conditional evaluation over all subjects. The estimate reports its Monte Carlo standard error and per-subject effective sample sizes.

source
NLMEEstimation.AdaptiveGaussHermite Type
julia
AdaptiveGaussHermite(; nodes = 5, max_points = 100_000)

Adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature of each subject's marginal log-likelihood: nodes points per random-effect dimension, as a tensor product, centred at the subject's conditional mode and scaled by its inverse curvature (computed as for ImportanceSampling). nodes = 1 is the Laplace approximation. A subject with d random effects costs nodes^d batched evaluation points; a subject needing more than max_points is refused.

NLMEEstimation.residual_diagnostics Function
julia
residual_diagnostics(model, fit; replicates = 1000, rng = Random.default_rng(),
                     progress = nothing)
residual_diagnostics(model, θ, σ, ω; subjects = 1:nsubjects(model), η0 = nothing,
                     replicates = 1000, rng = Random.default_rng(),
                     ebe_options = EBEOptions(), progress = nothing) -> ResidualDiagnostics

PRED, IPRED, IWRES, CWRES and NPDE for a model with GaussianObservations, at a fit's estimate or at given population coordinates. Subject modes (EBEs) warm-start from the fit's EBEs or final chain states, or from η0. Both NLMELayout and RaggedNLMELayout are supported.

CWRES follows Hooker, Staatz and Karlsson (2007): the observations of subject i are linearized around its EBE, giving mean μ(η̂) - J η̂ and covariance J Ω Jᵀ + diag(v(η̂)), and the residual is decorrelated by the Cholesky factor of that covariance's inverse. This is the approximation the FOCE(I) objective of focei_evaluate uses, with the residual variance at the EBE.

NPDE follows Brendel, Comets, Laffont, Laveille and Mentré (2006) and Comets, Brendel and Mentré (2008). replicates datasets are simulated at the population parameters (η ~ N(0, Ω), then y ~ N(μ(η), v(η))), each costing one batched observation_moments call. The observed and simulated vectors are decorrelated with the Cholesky factor of the inverse of each subject's simulated covariance. replicates must exceed every subject's number of observations; replicates = 0 skips the simulation.

progress (a Treebars node or backend symbol) gets a Residual diagnostics node with the subject modes and a Simulated replicates counter; an interrupt raises Treebars.ProgressInterrupted.

source
NLMEEstimation.ResidualDiagnostics Type
julia
ResidualDiagnostics

Per-observation residual diagnostics from residual_diagnostics. Every field below except subjects, modes and replicates holds one vector per subject in subjects, in observation order:

  • observations: y.

  • pred: population predictions, the observation means at η = 0.

  • ipred: individual predictions, the means at the subject's EBE η̂ (modes).

  • iwres: (y - ipred) / sqrt(v(η̂)), with v the observation variance.

  • cpred: the population mean of the first-order linearization around η̂, μ(η̂) - J η̂ with J = ∂μ/∂η at η̂.

  • cwres: conditional weighted residuals: U (y - cpred). Here U is the upper Cholesky factor of the inverse of the linearized covariance C = J Ω Jᵀ + diag(v(η̂)), so UᵀU = C⁻¹ and cwres is decorrelated within the subject.

  • cwres_per_observation: (y - cpred) ./ sqrt.(diag(C)), each observation standardized without decorrelation; this is what nlmixr2 reports as CWRES.

  • epred, npd, npde: simulation-based diagnostics over replicates datasets simulated at the population parameters. epred is the simulated mean, and npd the normal quantile of the prediction discrepancy, the fraction of simulated values below y. npde is the same after decorrelation by the Cholesky factor of the inverse simulated covariance, as for cwres. pde is the decorrelated discrepancy before the quantile. A discrepancy of 0 or 1 is replaced by 1/(2K) or 1 - 1/(2K) for K replicates. These fields are nothing when replicates = 0.

Under a correct model, cwres and npde are approximately independent standard normal values.
